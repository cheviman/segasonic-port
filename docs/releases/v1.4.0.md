The native port of Sega's 1993 arcade game *SegaSonic The Hedgehog*: not an emulator, but the game's own
program decompiled into C ([segasonic-decomp](https://github.com/cheviman/segasonic-decomp)) and built for
Windows, Linux and macOS, and, with its assets made on your computer, for the Nintendo Switch, Android and
Anbernic's handhelds.

**No game data is included.** You need your own MAME ROM set: `sonic.zip`, *SegaSonic The Hedgehog*
(Japan, rev. C). Put it beside the game, or drop it on the window the game opens the first time: the game
checks it and takes out what it uses, once.

What is new since v1.3.1:

- **MULTIPLAYER**, the title menu's new line: the same game on up to three devices of the local network, a
  player each, any of them together (computers, phones, a Switch, a handheld). One picks HOST, the others JOIN
  and its room; NAME is the name the room shows. The host's START begins the game on every device at once,
  with the host's DIFFICULTY, VITALITY, CONTINUES and WIDE, and the others join at player select with START,
  as at a cabinet. Every device runs the same frames with every player's controls
  ([arcade-lan](https://github.com/cheviman/arcade-lan) in lockstep, as TMNT's): a slow network shows as the
  game holding (WAITING FOR the device's name), never as the games parting. Back leaves the game and the
  others play on; the game over brings everyone back to MULTIPLAYER. The devices have to be this same version.
- **VSYNC** (OPTIONS → VIDEO, on at first, on the computers): each frame at the screen's refresh, with no
  tearing. The game keeps its own pace: a 120 Hz screen shows a frame twice, and a frame a frame late is left
  out, so neither the game nor its sound waits for the screen.
- **WIDE 16:10** (OPTIONS → VIDEO: OFF, 16:10, 16:9), for the Steam Deck's 1280 x 800: the same wider stages,
  the window's middle 358 columns. On Android a tablet's screen takes 16:10 by itself.
- **Game controllers** (the computers and Android, from TMNT's players' reports on an Nvidia Shield):
  - The games carry [SDL_GameControllerDB](https://github.com/mdqinc/SDL_GameControllerDB), the community's
    mappings, given to SDL before it looks for controllers, so one plugged in before the game starts is a game
    controller from the start. A `gamecontrollerdb.txt` of yours still goes over them.
  - A TV's remote (Android TV's) no longer takes player 1 from a game controller.
  - The log (`segasonic.log` on Android) says what SDL finds of each controller and the first keys and buttons
    pressed, for a player to send.
- **Touch controls**: no larger on a tablet than on a big phone (their size has a top of its own in inches,
  as TMNT's), and their names inside their buttons.
  Android's back goes on from the pause, as CONTINUE does.
- **@cheviman** over the title's ©SEGA 1992, and the version at the bottom right of OPTIONS.
- **The Windows executables** carry their version information (Properties → Details: cheviman, SegaSonic,
  1.4.0), and the 24 MB of memory the game loads its assets into is taken when it loads them, no longer
  reserved, empty, in the executable's image. v1.3.1's `SegaSonic.exe` and `SegaSonic Arcade.exe` were marked
  by one antivirus of VirusTotal's 68 (MaxSecure, a generic heuristic, "susgen"; the other 67 found nothing):
  an executable without version information and with a large, empty section is what such heuristics take
  for a packed one. No executable carries the ROM, now or before; what the game runs from is your own
  `segasonic.assets`.

The Switch's `.nsp` and the Android APK are made again with this release's `SegaSonic NSP` and
`SegaSonic APK` (their `switch` and `android` folders hold the new game); an APK made with the same key
installs over the last one and keeps what the game wrote. The APK now asks for the network (MULTIPLAYER).
What they make, and the Anbernic folder with your pack in it, carries your ROM's assets: it is yours alone,
never share it.

How it was checked: MULTIPLAYER with two of the console's headless builds on one computer, one HOSTing and one
JOINing through the menus at 60 frames a second, the second player joining at player select: the two states
were the same, frame by frame, for 2495 frames of the game; and with the second one leaving with back
mid-game, the first played on alone and the second was back in MULTIPLAYER. The README's pictures of the
stages are the same pixel for pixel as v1.3.1's. Windows, Android, the Switch and the Anbernic were built on a
computer, Linux and macOS by GitHub Actions. **Not played yet on a device**: MULTIPLAYER between two
computers, a phone or a Switch on a real network (and Windows' firewall's question the first time), VSYNC on a
120 Hz screen, WIDE 16:10 on a Steam Deck, the controllers on an Nvidia Shield.

SDL2 and the C runtime are built into the computers' executables: nothing else to install. The macOS apps
are not signed by a developer: the first time, open one with right click → Open (on macOS 15 and later, try
to open it, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway).

Controls, settings, MULTIPLAYER, the Switch, Android, Anbernic's handhelds and where the game keeps its files:
