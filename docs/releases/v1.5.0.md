The native port of Sega's 1993 arcade game *SegaSonic The Hedgehog*: not an emulator, but the game's own
program decompiled into C ([segasonic-decomp](https://github.com/cheviman/segasonic-decomp)) and built for
Windows, Linux, macOS and the Raspberry Pi, and, with its assets made on your computer, for the Nintendo
Switch, Android and Anbernic's handhelds, and for iPhone and iPad, which take the ROM set themselves, see
below.

**No game data is included.** You need your own MAME ROM set: `sonic.zip`, *SegaSonic The Hedgehog*
(Japan, rev. C). Put it beside the game, or drop it on the window the game opens the first time: the game
checks it and takes out what it uses, once.

What is new since v1.4.1:

- **The music at its own speed**: with samples playing (the voices, the drums), the music ran about 9% slower
  than on MAME. The sound program refills a sample's wave memory 256 bytes at a time, and the original Z80
  takes the timer's interrupt between any two of them; the decompiled copy ran them as one step, longer than
  the timer's period, so a tick of the music's tempo was lost at every refill. The copy takes its interrupts
  between bytes now, as the Z80 does: measured against MAME 0.261, the timer's interrupts and the notes per
  10 seconds are MAME's.
- **SDL 3 on every platform that has it** (the Anbernic keeps its firmware's SDL 2): the window port is
  written for SDL 3.4.20: the window, the sound, the joysticks and the mice (the arcade game's separate
  trackballs too) are SDL 3's.
- **iPhone and iPad**: an unsigned `.ipa` in `segasonic-ios.zip`, signed by the player's sideloading tool
  (AltStore, SideStore, Sideloadly, TrollStore). Put `sonic.zip` in the app's folder in the Files app; the
  first start takes the assets out of it. The console game with touch controls, landscape, the picture
  between the notch's sides, the home indicator dimmed, the sound heard with the ringer off.
- **The Raspberry Pi 3 and later**, with the 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS: `segasonic-raspberrypi-arm64.tar.gz`,
  both games, SDL 3 built in, the ROM set taken in the first time as on the other computers.
- **CONTINUE and EXIT in the console game's pause on every platform**: up and down and accept, a finger on a
  choice where there is a touch screen, and on a computer the mouse over a choice chooses it and a click
  picks it. EXIT ends the game as the title's EXIT does.
- **OPTIONS > VIDEO: INTEGER SCALE and FPS**: the picture a whole number of times its pixels, and the frames
  a second at the picture's top right, the game's own pixels with a shadow, and in the window's title. A game
  device has a VIDEO page of its own, PIXELS, INTEGER SCALE and FPS; its pixels are the player's now, smooth
  at first.
- **segasonic.log**, beside the game's settings, when the game is started with no console (from Explorer or a
  launcher, and always on the Switch, Android and iOS): what it says, with the screen's size and refresh and
  the sound's device. Attach it to a report.
- **The video drawn about a third faster**, every picture the same as before (2.04 ms a frame on one thread
  before, 1.25 ms now): room for the Raspberry Pi and the handhelds.

The Switch's `.nsp` and the Android APK are made again with this release's `SegaSonic NSP` and
`SegaSonic APK` (their `switch` and `android` folders hold the new game); an APK made with the same key
installs over the last one and keeps what the game wrote. What they make, and the Anbernic folder with your
pack in it, carries your ROM's assets: it is yours alone, never share it.

**Not tried yet on a device**: the iPhone and iPad app (built by the CI, never run on a real one), the
Raspberry Pi (run under emulation, not on a Pi), and the Switch, Android and Anbernic builds with this
release's SDL 3 (the Anbernic's through a layer over its firmware's SDL 2): built and checked, not yet played
on the device. If you have one of these, please report what you see, with `segasonic.log`.

SDL 3 and the C runtime are built into the computers' executables: nothing else to install. The macOS apps
are not signed by a developer: the first time, open one with right click → Open (on macOS 15 and later, try
to open it, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway).
